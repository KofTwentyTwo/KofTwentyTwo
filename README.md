# James Maes

Technology executive, SaaS founder, and former CEO with 25+ years building
products, engineering organizations, and the systems businesses rely on.
My work connects product direction and business priorities with architecture
and execution.

Today, as CTO of Greater Goods and its [Me.Health](https://www.me.health)
division, my remit spans software, cloud, firmware, and engineering operations
for connected healthcare products. Alongside that work, I'm the founder of
[QRun-IO](https://qrun.io) and remain hands-on with application platforms,
developer tooling, and open-source engineering.

## Business perspective, engineering depth

As founder and CEO of Infoplus, I bootstrapped a warehouse and order management
SaaS business from inception to a 2021 exit, reaching $4M+ ARR. At ColdTrack,
I led a 45+ person technology organization and a $6M+ annual technology budget
during the company's growth from $25M to $150M+ in annual revenue.

Those roles combined product strategy, P&L ownership, engineering leadership,
platform modernization, and acquisition integration. Hands-on engineering helps
me understand how a system works; operating experience helps me decide what it
needs to accomplish.

## Engineering focus

- **Product direction and architecture:** translate business needs into useful
  products and systems that can evolve with them.
- **Teams and delivery:** develop engineering leaders, clarify ownership, and
  establish practices that help teams deliver reliably.
- **Technology investment:** connect modernization, cloud and data platforms,
  and acquisition integration to business priorities.
- **AI adoption:** explore where AI improves engineering and product workflows,
  with explicit boundaries, evidence, and human accountability.

## Selected engineering work

| Project | What it demonstrates |
| --- | --- |
| [QQQ](https://github.com/QRun-IO/qqq) | Platform architecture: a metadata-driven Java framework that brings application models, processes, and interfaces together. |
| [Standards](https://github.com/KofTwentyTwo/standards) | Engineering governance: development and security practices backed by repository checks and reusable delivery workflows. |
| [Munitor](https://github.com/KofTwentyTwo/Munitor) | Repeatable delivery: shared CI/CD configuration for building, testing, packaging, and deploying applications. |
| [AppKit](https://github.com/KofTwentyTwo/AppKit) | Reusable foundations: shared Windows application services and adapters that carry common responsibilities across products. |

These projects connect platform design with the practices needed to maintain
and ship software. The [Windows app template](https://github.com/KofTwentyTwo/windows-app-template)
puts AppKit and the shared delivery conventions together in a project starter.

Smaller applications make the same approach tangible:
[gclo](https://github.com/KofTwentyTwo/gclo) manages an organization's GitHub
repositories, and [MarkdownViewr](https://github.com/KofTwentyTwo/MarkdownViewr.Windows)
provides a focused Windows Markdown reading experience.
